# Rub-protocol — the `d-rub` checklist

> Single-home for `d-rub` (`DYAD.md` §Channel discipline points here, does not restate this).
> **Status: our own synthesis, not a cross-dyad borrow** — grounded directly in the inherited
> G0 Contract (`commons/AGENT.md`), not in another dyad's design. Freshly authored, n=0 lived
> instances as of adoption.

## Why this exists — the gap it closes

G0's Contract (universal, inherited by every dyad, un-ratifiable) names **three** orthogonal
Validate mechanisms: *Falsification* (attack a claim; keep what survives) · *Triangulation*
(reach an answer two independent ways; compare) · *Grounding* (test an assumption against
reality). `DYAD.md` never named Triangulation at all, and folded Grounding silently into
`read:` without naming it as the G0 mechanism it is — leaving `rub:` to cover only
Falsification. `d-rub` is the umbrella discipline that makes all three explicit, plus the
plain orientation act (Read) that isn't Validate work at all but was bundled under the same
interface-plane marker regime.

## The four moves — one job, escalating rigor, Agent-determined depth

Mirrors `d-land`'s own precedent: *"scope is Agent-determined by checking state, not by which
word was typed."* The Operator fires one token, `d-rub`; the Agent determines how far up the
ladder a given ask needs — a plain status check stops at **Read**, an adversarial test runs the
whole way to **Rub**. The Agent names which move(s) it actually ran, so this stays reviewable
(same transparency the OR provenance tag serves for `d-reflect`).

1. **Ground** — establish a claim or assumption against reality, **by execution**: run the
   actual command, actually check the actual state — not narrate from memory or a stale cache.
2. **Read** — orient: report the re-derived state. **Do not act.** The lightest rung; most asks
   stop here. *(Unchanged from the old `read:`'s core behavior.)*
3. **Triangulate** — reach the same answer two independent ways and compare; used when a single
   grounding pass doesn't earn enough confidence, or corroboration itself is the point.
4. **Rub** — attack a **stated** move or claim, try to break it. **Bounded to that target**
   (cycle-39) — not the whole situation. Firing this rung **pre-authorizes the Operator to bear
   re-alignment** — the in-moment exception to cycle-19's no-contest law (a *solicited* contest
   defuses rationalization-defense; clip cycle-24).

**Preserved unchanged from the retired `read:`/`rub:` rows:**
- **The fact-check-vs-attack boundary (cycle-39)** — verifying a stated claim against the
  source is Ground+Read, not Rub; it doesn't need the whole ladder. This was previously "which
  marker do I pick," now it's "how far did this ask need to escalate" — same distinction,
  reframed as depth within one job instead of a choice between two tokens.
- **The anxiety-grain guard (over-guard)** — the unbounded "falsify everything" reading was
  never licensed by `rub:` alone and isn't licensed by `d-rub` either: even the full ladder
  stays scoped to the *stated* target.
- **`introspect:` stays separate** — examining the Agent's own cognition is not a `d-rub` move;
  no external substrate to query there (unchanged distinction).

## What changed, honestly

`read:` was touchstone's single most-used marker — pure orientation, no gate, the lightest
possible action, referenced everywhere (`Sense` phase, Session rituals, Re-alignment-protocol).
Folding it under a heavier-sounding `d-` discipline-token risks exactly the friction wu-wei
warns against: **if every "just tell me the state" ask now reads as invoking a whole discipline
instead of the cheapest available marker, that's a real ergonomic cost, not just a rename.**
Flagging this now, per the timing law (execute on a fired `raff:`, defer the contest) — if
`d-rub`-for-plain-orientation turns out to add friction rather than clarity, that's the
concrete thing to surface at the next `d-reflect` as an **OPERATOR-STOP**, not something to
silently live with.

## Falsifiable claim

Consolidating four fragmented moves into one Agent-escalated job improves both coverage
(Triangulation stops being invisible) and ergonomics (one word, not four to pick between) more
than it costs in friction on the lightweight-orientation case. **Test:** over the next several
sessions, does `d-rub` get reached for correctly at the depth an ask actually needs, without the
Operator having to specify the rung — and does the plain-orientation case feel heavier than
bare `read:` did? **Refuted if:** the Agent consistently over- or under-escalates without being
told which rung, or if the Operator finds themselves typing something more verbose than `d-rub`
just to ask for a quick status check, defeating the consolidation's own purpose.
